Eight rows, eight exact, all EH-bearing, all on the first compile. The
lane arrived here with three unclaimed 33-byte wrappers named as leads;
reading the compiland out turned them into eight.

IDENTIFICATION. The carve from 0x46b610 to 0x46df80 is fourteen
functions, and it maps one-for-one onto the Dreamcast roster's own line
order with no freedom left, because BOTH ENDS are already pinned:
TCombatHeroSubWindow::Show/UnShow at 0x46cdf0/0x46ce70 were claimed from
their bodies, and 0x46df80 was proved to be TCombatCreatureSubWindow's
UnShow from combatManager::DoCommand. Between them the five classes
repeat one shape - a large constructor, a 33-byte wrapper, a destructor -
in the roster's order, and every wrapper's single call target is the
destructor 0x30 bytes later.

The two derived names are read off their constructors, not guessed:
0x46bc30 passes "cbar.pcx" (0x670030) to the base and stores 0x63d420,
0x46c050 passes "coplacbr.pcx" (0x670054) and stores 0x63d430 - the two
tables that follow the base's 0x63d410 at the family's 16-byte stride.
That stride is itself the evidence for four virtual slots, which is
exactly the roster's virtual set (destructor, set_rollover,
set_rollover_buttons, DisableAllButtons).

TWO BODY SHAPES, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS LOAD-BEARING.

The first three destructors are the same 120 bytes as the bottom-view
family: the base body inlined whole - vptr store, widget-removal loop,
??1TSubWindow call - with the derived class's own vptr store killed as
dead. So none of the three names its own table.

The last two are 107 bytes, and the 13-byte difference is one missing
call: their loop deletes each widget but does NOT hand it to
parentWindow->RemoveWidget first. They also DO name their own tables
(0x63d440 and 0x63d444). Both facts have the same cause - these two
derive straight from TSubWindow, whose destructor is an out-of-line call
rather than an inlined body, so VC6 cannot prove the derived vptr store
dead and keeps it.

AND THAT SURVIVING STORE IS WORTH FOUR ROWS. The standing rule from last
round is that a derived ??_G cannot be claimed without its constructor,
because with the vptr store dead nothing in the TU references the derived
table and VC6 emits neither it nor the wrapper. The rule holds here for
TCombatControlSubWindow and TCombatPlacementSubWindow, whose wrappers are
parked as BLOCKED one-liners. But it does NOT bind whenever a destructor
keeps its store - the family base, TCombatHeroSubWindow and
TCombatCreatureSubWindow each reference their own table from their own
destructor, so all three wrappers came back claimed and 100.00 with no
constructor anywhere. That is a cheaper unblocking condition than the one
this lane was handed, and it is measured, not argued.

NO DERIVED FIELD IS NAMED. 0x4721d0 allocates TCombatPlacementSubWindow
at 0x3c and TCombatControlSubWindow at 0x40; the base constructor writes
one dword at +0x34, the control constructor writes +0x38 and +0x3c, and
the placement constructor writes neither - which leaves the +0x38 dword
attributable either to the base or to each derived class. The header says
so and stops there. None of the eight bodies needs it: every one touches
only the inherited TSubWindow subobject.
